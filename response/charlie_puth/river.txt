"River" is a song by Charlie Puth that explores the relationship between two people. In the first verse, Puth sings to his partner, urging them not to be cruel and push him away. He doesn't want to get hurt, so he wants them to open up and be honest with him. In the pre-chorus, he suggests that they should fall in love and be open to a different perspective. The chorus repeats the refrain "don't run" and implores the river (a metaphor for the relationship) not to run from their love.

In the second verse, Puth continues to plead with his partner not to give up on their relationship when times get tough. He reassures them that his heart is their home and that there is nothing as cold as running on your own. The bridge repeats the refrain "don't run from me, river" before returning to the pre-chorus and chorus.

The song has a catchy chorus and relatable lyrics about the ups and downs of relationships. Puth's emotional vocals help to convey the message of the song and make it feel sincere. Overall, "River" is a well-written and catchy pop song that explores the complexities of love and relationships.
