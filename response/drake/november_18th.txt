"November 18th" by Drake is a tribute to the late DJ Screw, the Houston-based producer who pioneered the slowed-down, chopped-and-screwed style of hip-hop. The song is a reflective look at Drake's relationship with Screw's sound and the city of Houston, and features plenty of references to the city's culture and music. 

The song begins with Drake reflecting on the people he has surrounded himself with and the impact they have had on his life, noting that they would do anything for him. He then proceeds to talk about meeting a girl from Houston and how he is a "realer" than anyone she has ever met. 

The chorus of the song reflects Drake's feelings of being "up so high" and feeling like everything is moving in slow motion. He is in his zone, sipping on drinks and getting ready for a night of passion. 

The second verse sees Drake reflecting on his own shortcomings, admitting that he doesn't give his partners the time they deserve. He is aware of his own faults, but still proceeds to make the most of the situation, taking it slow and enjoying the moment. 

The bridge of the song sees Drake reminiscing about Houston, talking about the candy-painted cars and bumping DJ Screw in the parking lot. The song is an ode to the city and its influence on Drake's life and music. 

Overall, "November 18th" is a heartfelt tribute to the city of Houston and its influence on Drake's life and music.
