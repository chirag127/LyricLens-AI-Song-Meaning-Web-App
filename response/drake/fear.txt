"Fear" by Drake is a song that examines the effects of fame, fortune, and success on the artist's life. It is a candid exploration of the fear of losing touch with those closest to him, coupled with the feeling of isolation that comes with being a celebrity. In the opening verse, Drake expresses his frustration with the industry, noting that despite his success, he is still the same person. He acknowledges that he plays the game and has to work hard to be successful, but that he never asked for the fame and recognition that comes with it.

The second verse delves deeper into Drake's feelings of being disconnected from his past as he talks about his "lil bro" from his hometown and expresses his desire to have equal opportunity for everyone. He talks about getting high to cope with the lows of life, and how he needs to stay true to himself in order to be successful.

The chorus serves as a plea for those closest to him to not be scared of him and to remember the good times they had together before fame and fortune changed everything. He reflects on his feelings of loneliness and urges his fans not to believe the lies they hear about him.

The third verse sees Drake detailing the difficulty of being true to himself and his art while balancing the demands of the industry. He laments the pressure of having to defend his dreams and how the money has changed his life, leaving him feeling distant from his old friends and unable to connect with them.

The final verse sees Drake reflecting on the fact that money can indeed change everything. His uncle is absent from his life now, and he is fearful of what the future holds for him. He wonders if his relationships will ever be the same and acknowledges that his music has left him exposed.

In summary, "Fear" by Drake is an honest and heartfelt exploration of the fear of change and disconnection that comes with fame and success. The song reflects the artist's innermost thoughts and emotions, and his struggle to remain true to himself and those closest to him despite the pressures of the industry. Linguistically, the song uses a direct, conversational style to convey its message, with a heavy focus on personal pronouns to emphasize Drake's feelings of isolation. The emotion of fear is also evident in the song's use of imagery, such as the reference to his uncle and the line "people seem to notice every time I do smile". Overall, "Fear" is an emotionally charged exploration of the highs and lows of fame and success.
