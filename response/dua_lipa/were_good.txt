The song "We're Good" by Dua Lipa is an upbeat anthem about the end of a relationship. The song's lyrics speak to the idea of two people realizing that they are not meant to be together and that they need to let go of their grudges and be on their own. In the first verse, Lipa talks about how she is on an island, even when her partner is close, and how she'd rather be alone than take the silence. In the pre-chorus, Lipa acknowledges that she and her partner have given it their all and that it is time for them to part ways. 

In the chorus, Lipa sings about how the relationship is not meant to be and that it would be best for them to move on, but she also stresses that she won't judge her partner when they eventually find someone else. This line speaks to the idea of taking responsibility for the end of a relationship and respecting the other person's right to move on. 

In the second verse, Lipa encourages her partner to move on and get what they want, and she also reassures them that this won't be a burden if they don't hold a grudge. This is a powerful message of acceptance and understanding, as Lipa recognizes that her partner needs to do what is best for themselves. 

The chorus returns in the third verse, and the song ends with Lipa asking her partner to agree to go their separate ways without holding anything against her. 

Overall, "We're Good" is a powerful anthem about the end of a relationship. The lyrics speak to the idea of two people letting go of their grudges, taking responsibility for the end of the relationship, and respecting each other's right to move on. Additionally, the song offers a strong message of acceptance and understanding, as Lipa encourages her partner to do what is best for themselves and to not hold anything against her. 

From a linguistic analysis standpoint, the song's lyrics show the emotions of sadness, acceptance, and understanding. The song acknowledges the pain of a relationship ending and the need to let go, but it also offers hope for the future and encourages both parties to take responsibility and accept their separate paths.
