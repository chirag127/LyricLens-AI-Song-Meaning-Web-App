In her song "All to Myself," Dua Lipa explores themes of longing, desire, and commitment. The song begins with a feeling of isolation, as the narrator is surrounded by people in a crowded room, yet they only have eyes for each other. This is further emphasized with the line "it's just day two," implying a connection that has already been established between them, even after such a short amount of time. The narrator is captivated by this person, and expresses a desire to have them all to themselves. 

The chorus of the song drives this point home with lines like "I want you all to myself" and "I'm the one that gets you best," expressing both the narrator's desire to be the only one in the other person's life, and their confidence that they can provide that for them. The song also contains a powerful message of commitment, with the narrator saying goodbye to all the rest, implying that they are choosing to give their attention, love, and loyalty exclusively to the other person.

The song also contains a strong emotional component, with the music and lyrics conveying the narrator's feelings of longing and desire. The lyrics use repetition to enhance the emotional resonance, with the chorus repeating the lines "I want you all to myself" and "Only only one" multiple times. This repetition serves to emphasize the intensity of the narrator's feelings and their commitment to the other person.

In summary, "All to Myself" is an emotionally charged song about longing and commitment. Through the use of repetition and emotionally charged lyrics and music, the narrator expresses their desire to have the other person all to themselves, and the confidence that they can provide that for them. The song conveys a powerful message of commitment, and the listener is left with a feeling of love and devotion.
