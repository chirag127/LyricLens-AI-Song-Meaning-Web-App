In her song "Boys Will Be Boys," Dua Lipa takes a stand against the dangerous and damaging idea that boys should be excused for their misgivings simply because they are male. The song begins with a description of the dangerous environment that women are placed in due to the expectations placed on men. This is described in the lyrics "It's second nature to walk home before the sun goes down and put your keys between your knuckles when there's boys around/ Isn't it funny how we laugh it off to hide our fear/ when there's nothing funny here." Lipa emphasizes the danger that women face from men and their expectations of them, as well as the way that women often laugh off their fear in order to seem less vulnerable. 

The chorus of the song draws attention to the idea of "boys will be boys" and the fact that it is used as an excuse for male misgivings. In this section, Lipa sings "Boys will be boys will be boys will be boys will be boys/ But girls will be women." This is an important statement because it highlights how women are held to a higher standard and that "boys will be boys" is not an acceptable excuse for male behavior. 

The song continues with the lyrics "I'm sure if there's something that I can't find the words to say/ I know that there will be a man around to save the day/ And that was sarcasm in case you needed it mansplained/ I should've stuck to ballet." This section of the song further highlights the way that women are expected to rely on men and the way they are often dismissed when they voice their opinions. Lipa implies that she should have remained silent and stuck to something that is seen as more traditionally feminine. 

The pre-chorus and chorus are then repeated, with the lyrics "When will we stop saying things 'cause they're all listening/ No the kids ain't alright/ Oh and they do what they see 'cause it's all on TV/ Oh the kids ain't alright/ Boys will be boys will be boys will be boys will be boys/ But girls will be women." Here, Lipa highlights how the idea of "boys will be boys" is perpetuated in media and how this is damaging to children and young adults. 

The song ends with the lyrics "If you're offended by this song you're clearly doing something wrong/ If you're offended by this song then you're probably saying/ Boys will be boys will be boys will be boys will be boys/ But girls will be women." Here, Lipa implies that if anyone is offended by this song, it is because they are still holding onto the idea that boys will be boys. 

In summary, "Boys Will Be Boys" is a powerful song that challenges the idea that boys should be excused for their misgivings simply because they are male. The song highlights the dangers that this idea can bring and the way it is perpetuated in media. Through her lyrics, Dua Lipa calls for a shift in the way we think about gender roles and expectations and encourages us to hold everyone to the same high standards. 

The lyrics of the song include a great deal of emotion and social commentary, with a heavy focus on the idea of gender roles and expectations. The language she uses is full of power and strength, further emphasizing her message. Additionally, the use of sarcasm and irony throughout the song highlights the absurdity of the expectations placed on women. 

Overall, "Boys Will Be Boys" is an important and powerful song that emphasizes the need for gender equality and highlights the dangerous and damaging effects of the idea that boys should be excused for their misgivings. Dua Lipa's use of irony, sarcasm, and emotion make this an important and powerful song that deserves to be heard.
