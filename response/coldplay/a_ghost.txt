"A Ghost" by Coldplay is a song about the longing and sadness experienced when two people are separated by death or distance. The song begins with the line "they're calling you some kind of ghost", which introduces the idea of a ghost as a metaphor for the relationship between the two people as it is no longer alive and tangible. The line "but I know that's not true" suggests that the speaker is aware of the presence of the other person, even though they are no longer able to communicate or be together physically.

The chorus of the song offers a glimpse of hope, as the speaker acknowledges that "maybe I'll go, maybe you're safe". This line implies that the speaker is willing to brave the transition to the afterlife in order to be with the other person, but is also aware that the other person may be safe and have found peace. 

The bridge of the song serves as a plea for the other person to come over, a reminder of the speaker's longing for their presence. The line "I would love you to come over" is a plea for the other person to come and visit, even if it is only to say goodbye.

The song's lyrics are filled with emotion, longing, and sadness, and the vocal delivery is tinged with a sense of desperation and longing. The instrumentation is sparse, allowing the vocals to take center stage and amplify the sense of longing and sadness.

Overall, "A Ghost" is a beautiful and sorrowful song about the longing and sadness experienced when two people are separated by death or distance. The sparse instrumentation and emotive vocal delivery serve to amplify the emotion in the lyrics, creating a powerful and haunting song.
