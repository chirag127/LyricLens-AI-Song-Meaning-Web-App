Coldplay’s “Trouble (Live at KCRW)” is a song that explores the consequences of mistakes and the internal strife that can come from them. The lyrics paint a vivid picture of the singer being ensnared in a metaphorical spider web of guilt and regret, and how the singer is determined to redeem themselves and make amends for the wrongs they have done. 

The song begins with the singer reflecting on the “stupid things” they’ve said and done, and how they’ve found themselves in an inescapable web of their own making. In the chorus, the singer proclaims that they never meant to cause any trouble or do anyone wrong, but now they find themselves in this web of guilt and regret. 

The second verse continues the metaphor of the spider web, as the singer turns and twists in an attempt to escape the web, but finds themselves inescapably ensnared. The chorus reiterates the singer’s remorse and regret, and their determination to make amends for what they’ve done. 

The song ends with a repeated chorus of “they spun a web for me,” as the singer realizes that the web of guilt and regret was of their own making, and that they must accept the consequences of their actions. 

In terms of a linguistic analysis, the song is filled with strong emotions of regret and remorse. The lyrics are filled with repetition of the chorus, which further emphasizes the singer’s remorse and determination to make amends. The song is also filled with vivid imagery of the spider web, which further conveys the overwhelming feeling of guilt and regret that the singer is experiencing. 

Overall, Coldplay’s “Trouble (Live at KCRW)” is a powerful song that explores the consequences of mistakes and the internal strife that can come from them. By using vivid imagery and strong emotions, the song conveys the singer’s remorse and determination to make amends.
