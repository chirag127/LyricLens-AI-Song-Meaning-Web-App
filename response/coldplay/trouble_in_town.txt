"Trouble in Town" by Coldplay is a powerful indictment of systemic racism and injustice. The song paints a vivid picture of a broken system that creates and perpetuates inequality, highlighting how the law of the jungle or the law of the street can often be more powerful than the law of the land. It follows the story of an unnamed protagonist who faces discrimination and oppression, and is unable to escape it no matter what he does.

The first verse introduces the listener to the protagonist, who is facing "trouble in town" due to the injustice inflicted on his family. We also learn that there is "blood on the beat," a metaphor for the violence and injustice perpetuated by the system.

The second verse further develops the protagonist's story, as he is unable to find shelter or peace, and is instead met with more police. He is not given the same opportunities as others and is unable to escape the cycle of poverty and oppression. 

The bridge of the song is a conversation between a young man and a police officer. It is here that the protagonist realizes that the system is a broken one, and that it is perpetuating inequality and injustice. The officer's response is dismissive, and he tells the protagonist to stay in Jersey and not come back to Philadelphia.
