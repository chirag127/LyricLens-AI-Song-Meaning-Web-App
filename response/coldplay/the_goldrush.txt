The Goldrush by Coldplay is a song that tells a story of someone who is searching for gold in a valley near a mountain. The narrator is accompanied by their brother, with a bucket and a shovel, and a book about the color of coal. The song is full of imagery of their journey, and their struggles and determination to find gold. 

The lyrics paint a picture of the narrator going through a difficult journey, with a goal of finding gold. The chorus creates a sense of hope and determination, as they march through the cold in search of their dreams. There is also a sense of loneliness and isolation, as the narrator hears a tiny crackle on the telephone line, asking what use the metal is if it doesn’t shine. This line suggests that the narrator is searching for something more than just gold. 

The song speaks to the idea of hope and determination in the face of hardship and adversity. It also speaks to the idea of chasing your dreams and never giving up. The chorus serves as a reminder to never give up, no matter how hard the journey may be. 

In terms of linguistic analysis, the song reflects a sense of determination and perseverance. The lyrics are full of imagery, and the use of repetition in the chorus serves to emphasize the narrator’s determination. The song also uses metaphors, such as the “tiny crackle on the telephone line” and the “metal that doesn’t shine” to emphasize the narrator’s feelings of loneliness and isolation. 

Overall, The Goldrush is a song that speaks to the power of hope and determination in the face of adversity. It is a reminder to never give up on your dreams, and to keep marching through the cold, no matter how difficult the journey may be.
