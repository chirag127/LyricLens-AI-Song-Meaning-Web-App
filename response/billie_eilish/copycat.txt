In her song "Copycat," Billie Eilish paints the story of someone in her life who has been trying to imitate her and her lifestyle. In the opening verse, she calls out this person for their actions, telling them that they are her crime and that they shouldn't be kind or cautious when it comes to her. She then continues to describe the person in the second verse, comparing them to a silver dollar and a golden flame as well as describing them as dirty water and poison rain, implying that this person is bad news. In the pre-chorus, she reveals that they have been uninvited from her life due to their attempts to copy her.

The chorus of the song is a direct call-out to the individual in question, accusing them of trying to copy her manner, glamor, and everything else that makes her unique. She warns them to watch their back, as she can't watch theirs, and expresses her frustration that her uniqueness can't be shared.

In the third verse, Eilish takes a harsher tone, calling the person calloused and cold and telling them that she is in bold and they are in italic. She then goes on to warn them that they better love her because they are nothing but a clone. The pre-chorus follows, with Eilish reiterating her decision to uninvite them from her life for their attempts to copy her.

The bridge of the song is a direct message to the individual in question, telling them that she would hate to see them go but that they have crossed a line and run out of time. She expresses her sorrow at having to tell them, while also implying that they should have known better.

In summary, "Copycat" is a bold and powerful song that speaks to the importance of being unique, individual, and true to oneself. Eilish paints a vivid picture of someone in her life who has been trying to copy her and her lifestyle, and she calls them out for their actions. With its forceful lyrics and catchy beat, the song speaks to the importance of standing up for what you believe in and not letting anyone try to take advantage of you.

Linguistically, the song is very direct and to the point. Eilish uses strong and assertive language to make her point, with words like "crime," "trigger," "bold," and "clone" emphasizing her power and authority in the situation. She also uses a lot of first-person pronouns such as "I," "me," and "mine," which highlights her sense of ownership and entitlement over her life and her decisions. The song also contains a lot of repetition, with the chorus being repeated multiple times throughout the song and the pre-chorus also appearing twice. This repetition serves to both emphasize her point and create a catchy and memorable song.

Overall, "Copycat" is a powerful and empowering song that speaks to the importance of standing up for yourself and not letting anyone try to copy or take advantage of you. With its strong and assertive lyrics, the song sends a clear message that you should stay true to yourself and not let anyone take away your individuality.
