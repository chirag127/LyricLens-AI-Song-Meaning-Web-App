The song “All the Good Girls Go to Hell” by Billie Eilish is a dark, brooding look at how society judges good people. The song begins with the narrator singing about her inner demons, and how they are lonely, committing crimes, and unable to commit to anything. In the pre-chorus, the narrator talks about how the “hills burn in California”, and how her turn to ignore the world has come. This is the chorus where she talks about how “all the good girls go to hell”, because even God has enemies, and when heaven is out of sight, she will want the devil on her team. 

The second verse talks about how the narrator “looks at you needing me”, and how she is not a friend without some greenery. The pre-chorus then reiterates the chorus, with the narrator warning that “all the good girls go to hell”. The song ends with the narrator acknowledging that there is nothing left to save, and that she cannot do the “snowflake”. 

The song is a commentary on how society judges people, and how people are judged for doing the right thing. The narrator acknowledges that there are people who are viewed as “good”, but are still judged and punished by society. The song speaks to the difficult choices that people have to make, and how they can be punished for doing the right thing. The song also speaks to the loneliness that comes with being judged, and how it can be hard to find acceptance. 

The lyrics of the song are full of emotion and imagery, and the use of language helps to convey the narrator’s feelings of alienation and loneliness. The use of words such as “lonely”, “crime”, “vacation”, “fences”, “caving in”, and “saving” help to create a sense of isolation and despair. The use of imagery, such as the “hills burning in California”, helps to create an atmosphere of destruction and chaos. The use of metaphor in the lyrics also helps to convey the narrator’s feelings of being judged and punished for doing the right thing. 

Overall, “All the Good Girls Go to Hell” is a powerful and emotional song that speaks to the difficult choices people have to make and how they can be judged and punished for doing the right thing. The song speaks to the feelings of alienation and loneliness that come with being judged, and the use of language and imagery helps to convey these feelings in a powerful way.
