The song "Freedom" (2016 BET Awards) by Beyoncé is a powerful anthem of liberation and self-empowerment. The song is an ode to the struggles of African Americans and the fight for justice. Through the lyrics, Beyoncé conveys a message of resilience and determination to persevere through the obstacles of society.

The first verse of the song pays homage to Martin Luther King Jr., referencing the "promissory note" of the Constitution and Declaration of Independence. This verse speaks to the promise of freedom and justice that all people, regardless of race, should be guaranteed. 

In the second verse, Beyoncé speaks to her own journey of liberation. She speaks of "running blind" and the need to "riot through your borders" in order to find freedom. She also speaks of the need to "break chains" and not "let [her] freedom rot in hell." This verse is a powerful call to action, to fight for one's freedom and to never give up. 

The third verse features a collaboration with rapper Kendrick Lamar, who further emphasizes the need to fight for freedom and justice. He speaks of "40 acres" and "Lemonade all the time" in reference to the legacy of slavery in America, and of the need to fight for the liberation of African Americans. 

The final verse is Beyoncé's call to arms, to keep running in pursuit of freedom. She speaks of the need to "break chains all by [her]self" and to "keep running" even when faced with adversity and oppression. 

In summary, "Freedom" is a powerful anthem of liberation and self-empowerment. Beyoncé's lyrics are full of determination and resilience, conveying a message of hope and perseverance in the face of injustice. The song is a powerful call to action for African Americans to fight for their rights and freedom, and to never give up.

When it comes to the linguistic analysis of the song, the lyrics are filled with a sense of urgency, determination, and resilience. Beyoncé and Kendrick Lamar both use a direct and passionate tone in their lyrics, conveying a strong sense of self-belief and the need to fight for one's freedom. They both use the words "freedom" and "run" multiple times throughout the song, emphasizing the importance of perseverance and resilience. The lyrics also contain multiple references to slavery and the legacy of injustice, further emphasizing the need to fight for freedom and justice. 

Overall, "Freedom" is an inspiring anthem of liberation and resilience. The lyrics are full of passion and determination, conveying a powerful message of hope and perseverance in the face of adversity. This song is a powerful reminder of the need to fight for justice and freedom, and to never give up.
