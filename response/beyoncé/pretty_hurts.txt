Pretty Hurts by Beyoncé is a powerful song which explores the pressure women face to conform to societal standards of beauty. At the beginning of the song, Beyoncé is asked what her aspiration in life is, to which she responds that she wants to be happy. The song then goes on to explore the idea of how society puts unrealistic and often damaging expectations on women, and how this can be both physically and emotionally damaging.

The first verse discusses how society tells women that their physical appearance is the only thing that matters, “Mama said you’re a pretty girl, what’s in your head, it doesn’t matter, brush your hair, fix your teeth, what you wear is all that matters.” The chorus of the song reflects the idea that trying to meet these standards can be painful and damaging, “Pretty hurts, we shine the light on whatever’s worst, perfection is a disease of a nation.”

The second verse continues to explore this idea, discussing how the media and advertisements push unrealistic body standards on women, “Blonder hair, flat chest, TV says bigger is better, South Beach, sugar free, Vogue says thinner is better.” The chorus is repeated, emphasizing the idea that meeting these standards is painful and can be damaging.

The third verse discusses the idea of how trying to meet these standards can be damaging, “Ain’t got no doctor or pill that can take the pain away, the pain’s inside and nobody frees you from your body, it’s the soul that needs surgery.” The chorus is then repeated, emphasizing the idea that trying to meet these standards is damaging to the soul.

In summary, Pretty Hurts is a powerful song that explores the pressure women face to conform to unrealistic standards of beauty. The song speaks to the damaging physical and emotional effects that trying to meet these standards can have, and emphasizes the idea that it’s the soul that needs surgery, not physical appearance. 

From a linguistic analysis of the song lyrics, it is evident that Beyoncé expresses powerful emotions of pain, confusion, and frustration. The song also reflects a sense of empowerment and resilience in the face of societal pressures, as Beyoncé proclaims that she will “take the crown without falling down”.
