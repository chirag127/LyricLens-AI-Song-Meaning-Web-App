Ariana Grande's song, "Feminism (Quotes)", is a powerful anthem for female empowerment and gender equality. Through her lyrics, Grande explores the double standards and unfair expectations placed on women in society. She expresses her frustration with the double standard, noting that it is both boring and exhausting. Grande then responds to a commenter who said she was like a "whore" in her "Dangerous Woman" video on Facebook, and she speaks out against the incredible double standard that women face in the industry and in the world.

In the second verse, Grande calls out the assumption that women need to choose between makeup or their phone, saying that men are assuming that women have to make this choice. She then goes on to list some of the issues that need to be addressed in order to achieve gender equality, such as judgement, intolerance, meanness, double standards, misogyny, racism, and sexism.

In the third verse, Grande speaks out against the ignorance and meanness of people who comment on other people's bodies. She calls for people to respect body boundaries, and to encourage each other to feel like a "babe" no matter what shape or size they are.

In the fourth verse, Grande references Gloria Steinem's 1969 article, "After Black Power, Women's Liberation". She reflects on how, 46 years later, we still have a "long list of things" to change in order to achieve gender equality. 

The final verse of the song speaks out against the double standards when it comes to sex. Grande questions why a woman is labeled a "slut" for having sex, while a man is celebrated for the same behavior. She goes on to assert that people should be valued as individuals, rather than who they are seen with.
