Post Malone's song "A Thousand Bad Times" is a reflection on a tumultuous relationship and the struggles of having to move on from it. The song begins with Malone declaring that his partner wanted his car, but without her "face," she wouldn't get far. Despite his partner's flaws, Malone still professes his love for her. He acknowledges that he is wealthy and famous and that she knows this, but he insists that she does know him. He then reveals the cost of loving her and that despite her treating him poorly, he still comes back to her. 

The chorus reveals that despite the thousand bad times they have endured, it would take much more than that to kill him. He then states that he needs a place to sleep and that his partner's mother is crazier than her, implying that his partner is also unstable. Malone then reveals that he should leave but still wants more, comparing their relationship to a game. 

In the second verse, Malone reveals that he needs thicker armor in order to spend another night with her, and that it is the law of attraction that keeps him coming back. The song ends with Malone repeating the chorus, which serves as a reminder that it would take much more than a thousand bad times for him to be killed. 

The song serves as a powerful reflection on the struggles of dealing with an unhealthy relationship. Through his lyrics, Malone emphasizes the emotional toll that such a relationship can take on a person and the difficulties of having to move on from it. Linguistic analysis of the song's lyrics reveals a range of emotions, including anger, sadness, and resignation. 

Overall, "A Thousand Bad Times" is a powerful song that delves deep into the struggles of dealing with a tumultuous relationship. Through his lyrics, Malone paints a vivid picture of the emotional toll such a relationship can take on a person, while also emphasizing the difficulty of moving on from it.
