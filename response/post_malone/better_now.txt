"Better Now" by Post Malone is a song about heartache and moving on. In the song, Malone reflects on the end of a relationship, and how he is trying to come to terms with the situation. The song is structured as a back-and-forth between Post Malone and his former lover, with the chorus repeating the line: “you prolly think that you are better now, better now, you only say that 'cause I'm not around, not around.” 

The song paints a vivid picture of the pain of a relationship ending, and the conflicting emotions that come with it. Malone is grappling with the reality of the situation, and is trying to accept it and move on. He expresses regret at what could have been, and acknowledges that his former lover is likely better off without him, even though it hurts to admit it. He reflects on how much his life has changed since the relationship ended, and how he is struggling to move on.

The lyrics of the song contain a variety of linguistic devices that convey the emotions of the song. The repetition of certain words and phrases, such as “better now” and “not around”, emphasize the difficulty of the situation and the longing for what could have been. The use of alliteration in the lines “you know I never meant to let you down, let you down” creates a sense of sadness and regret. 

The song is an emotional and powerful track about the pain of a relationship ending, and the difficulty of moving on. The lyrics are full of imagery that conveys the feelings of heartache, and the emotions of the song are further enhanced by the use of linguistic devices. The song is a powerful and touching track, and serves as a reminder that sometimes, it's better to move on.
