In Post Malone's song "Rich & Sad", he reflects on a relationship that he tried to buy back with money and material possessions, but ultimately failed to do so. The song starts with Post Malone talking about his money and possessions, including a "hunnid thousand for the chain" and a car that he "chop[s] the top". This is juxtaposed with the chorus, where Post Malone reflects on how he is unable to buy back love, singing "I just keep on wishin' that the money made you stay". 

Throughout the song, Post Malone reflects on his loneliness, singing "I got a hundred big places but I'm still alone". This line is symbolic of his wish to fill the emptiness in his life with material possessions, but ultimately being unable to do so. With the chorus, Post Malone shows a deep understanding of the concept of love, and the fact that it can't be bought with money. 

The song is composed of a mix of rap verses and melody-driven choruses, creating a powerful contrast between the two. The rap verses provide a tone of bravado and pride in his wealth, while the chorus highlights his sadness and regret. This contrast is further reinforced by the use of vocal effects and production techniques, such as reverb and auto-tune. 

The lyrics of "Rich & Sad" provide a fascinating insight into the emotions and struggles of Post Malone. The song highlights the loneliness that comes with having wealth and material possessions, as well as showcasing Post Malone's understanding of the power of love. The juxtaposition of the rap verses and the chorus creates a powerful emotional contrast, with the chorus providing an emotional release after the rap verses. 

Overall, "Rich & Sad" is a powerful and insightful song from Post Malone. The lyrics provide an emotional exploration of the loneliness and sadness that comes with having wealth, and the chorus highlights the power of love, and how it cannot be bought with money. The production of the song further enhances the emotional range of the piece, making it one of Post Malone's most powerful songs.
