The song “Otherside” by Post Malone is an emotional and reflective piece that dives deep into the feelings of loss and longing. The song follows a protagonist that is struggling to cope with the absence of someone they love, and the lyrics explore the idea of trying to move on while being held back by the pain of remembering what once was. 

The song begins with the protagonist feeling like they are “waitin’ wide awake for the waves to come and take [them] right to you”, indicating that they are trying to find a way to get back to their loved one. The lines “one hundred bottles of the good shit couldn’t even bring you back” are a metaphor for how no matter how much the protagonist tries to escape the pain of their loss, it will never bring their loved one back. The chorus then reflects on the idea of “puttin’ on for you like a jersey”, emphasizing how the protagonist was willing to give everything to their loved one. 

The second verse then focuses on the protagonist trying to find solace in other people, but they are “circlin’ a drain” and “losin’ hope” as they can’t replace the person they loved. The chorus then reflects on the idea of how “everybody dies alone” and how that is a fact of life that the protagonist is coming to terms with. 

The bridge then reflects on how the protagonist’s back is “up against the wall”, emphasizing how they are feeling trapped and helpless. The protagonist feels like they are “used to losin’ hope”, suggesting a feeling of resignation. The chorus then ends with the protagonist asking if their loved one is “on the other side”, questioning if they will be saved or not. 

Overall, “Otherside” is a reflective and emotional song that dives deep into the feelings of loss and longing. The lyrics explore the protagonist’s struggle to move on while grappling with the pain of remembering what once was. The song also references the idea of how “everybody dies alone”, emphasizing the fact of life that the protagonist is learning to accept. Through its raw and honest lyrics, the song provides a powerful and poignant reflection on the human experience.
