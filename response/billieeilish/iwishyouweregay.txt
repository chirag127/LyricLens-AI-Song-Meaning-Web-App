"Wish You Were Gay" by Billie Eilish is a song about a person who is struggling to understand why their romantic partner is not interested in them. The song's narrator expresses their frustration and hurt at being rejected, and wishes that their partner was gay so that they could have a concrete explanation for why they are not interested.

In the first verse, the narrator describes how they feel "six words you never understood" and how they "never let you go." These lines suggest that the narrator has been trying to communicate with their partner and make them understand their feelings, but the partner has not been receptive. The use of the number six in this line could also be a reference to the six letters in the word "gay," foreshadowing the later lyrics.

The chorus of the song repeats the line "I just kinda wish you were gay," expressing the narrator's desire for a clear explanation for their partner's lack of interest in them. The use of the word "kinda" suggests that the narrator does not actually wish for their partner to be gay, but rather wishes for a clear reason for why they are not interested.

In the second verse, the narrator continues to express their frustration and hurt, mentioning how their partner "never made it there" and how they have been eating alone. These lines suggest that the narrator's partner has not been present for them emotionally or physically, leading to feelings of isolation and loneliness.

The bridge of the song sees the narrator addressing their partner directly, asking them to "spare my pride" and give an explanation for their lack of interest. The narrator also expresses their own selfishness and helplessness in the face of rejection.

Overall, "Wish You Were Gay" is a song about the pain and confusion of being rejected by someone you care about. The narrator wishes for a clear explanation for their partner's lack of interest, but ultimately realizes that the reason may not matter as much as the hurt that they are feeling.
